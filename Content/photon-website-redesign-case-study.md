## Overview

Prospective clients who had already met Photon through sales wanted more than the corporate deck, and the website gave them the same pitch again. I redesigned the site's structure and its five key page types so a buyer could see how Photon works with clients, not only what it builds.

## My Role

The site had to sell to clients, recruit talent, and express Photon's line, "We build relationships, not just products," without turning into a longer sales pitch. Most of the work was deciding what each audience needed to see, and in what order.

What I did:

- Ran a persona workshop and mapped each persona's journey using a "Lean Forward with Micro Lean Back Elements" engagement model
- Restructured the site's information architecture to match those journeys and the business goals
- Prioritized features against strategic goals instead of designing everything at once
- Defined the KPIs the redesign would be measured against
- Iterated on mockups for the home, services, case study, sales landing, and careers pages

## The Challenge

Photon's site worked as a corporate overview. Clients past the first conversation needed depth on services and proof of how Photon works, and the site had little of either. The redesign also had to carry the relationship message through content and layout, since a tagline alone would not do it.

---

## The Problem

**The site stopped at the pitch.** Potential clients got a summary of services, with no deeper content once they were interested.

**The relationship message lived in a tagline.** Nothing in the structure or content showed Photon's long-term partnership approach.

**Multiple audiences, one path.** Clients, candidates, and sales leads all landed on the same experience.

**Navigation did not match how people explore.** Content existed but was hard to reach in a logical order.

*Data point needed: any analytics, stakeholder feedback, or audit finding that backs these up.*

## Approach

**Start with the people, not the pages.** I built personas in a workshop around behaviors, needs, motivations, and frustrations, then mapped journeys from first contact to long-term engagement. The maps showed which touchpoints mattered, and the architecture followed from them.

**Structure before screens.** I reworked the information architecture before drawing the key pages, so the home page and templates sat on a structure that already made sense for each audience.

**Cut the feature list, then set the scorecard.** I prioritized features against strategic goals, then set KPIs for brand identity, thought leadership, conversion rates, and talent acquisition. Setting measures before mockups kept the design reviews tied to goals.

**Plan for the relationship message in content.** The relationship positioning shaped the content strategy too: value-driven content to show thought leadership and real client partnership, in place of more service copy.

## The Solution

**Architecture built on audience journeys.** A restructured site map that guides each audience through Photon's story and services in order.

**Templates for proof.** A case study template and a services page that give prospective clients depth after first contact.

**A dedicated sales landing page and careers page.** Separate entry points for clients and for candidates.

**A home page that invites exploration.** Interactive elements and a clean layout built to lead visitors deeper into the site.

**Consistent visual identity.** One look and feel across pages to support recognition and trust.
